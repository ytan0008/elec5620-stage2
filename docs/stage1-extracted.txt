1 Introduction
1.1 Background
Large language models (LLMs) now make it possible for one person to run a company
supported by specialised AI agents that plan, decide, use tools and collaborate. This
project applies that “One-Person AI Company” idea to education.
Many working professionals need an English certification such as IELTS or TOEFL
for university admission, visa applications or promotion. Good tutoring is expensive,
inflexible and inconsistent, and few tutors give detailed feedback on all four skills (Speak-
ing, Writing, Reading and Listening). Learners therefore follow inefficient study paths
and often sit the exam more than once.
1.2 Proposed Solution
We propose an AI-powered exam preparation platform run by a single human instructor
(the founder) and four specialised AI agents:
4

--- PAGE 6 ---
•Learning Coordinator Agent:onboards learners, builds their profile, creates
and adapts a personalised learning plan, recommends materials and manages hand-
offs between agents.
•Tutor Agent:runs speaking, writing, reading and listening practice, generates
prompts and gives immediate, detailed feedback.
•Assessment & Analytics Agent:scores work consistently against the official
rubrics, identifies weaknesses and error patterns, and predicts exam readiness.
•Operations & Quality Manager:checks that agent outputs are consistent and
confident, detects curriculum gaps and escalates problem cases to the human in-
structor.
Company brief.For working professionals preparing for IELTS or TOEFL, reliable
feedback on all four skills is expensive, slow and inconsistent because it depends on hu-
man tutors. Our one-person AI company coordinates a Learning Coordinator, a Tu-
tor, an Assessment & Analytics agent and an Operations & Quality Manager to deliver
personalised, quality-checked exam preparation, while the human founder approves low-
confidence or contradictory results.

2.2.1 Practising an IELTS/TOEFL Essay and Getting Feedback I Can Trust
(Naratorn Pisedtasalasai)
Scenario.Mai is an accountant who needs IELTS band 7 to apply for a job overseas.
Writing is her weakest skill, and a private tutor who marks essays costs too much and
takes days to reply. She wants to practise a Task 2 essay tonight and know straight away
what she is doing wrong.
She asks the platform for a practice question and gets one in the same style as the
real exam. She has 40 minutes to write about 250 words. After she submits, comments
start appearing within a few seconds: a wrong tense, a vague topic sentence, the word
“important” used five times. Each comment says why it is a problem and shows a better
version. She also sees an estimated band for each of the four writing criteria.
Mai never sees a result that contradicts itself. Behind the scenes, the system compares
the comments with the band scores. If they do not match, or the AI is not sure of its
marking, the essay goes to the human instructor first, and Mai is told it is being double-
checked. Once she has her feedback, she rewrites the weak paragraphs and submits again,
and the system shows her what improved since her first draft.
Ad hoc requirements.

practice activities but is still unsure whether she is ready for the real examination. She

2.2.4 Spotting a Curriculum Gap Before It Wastes Everyone’s Time(Mo-
hamed Azim)
Scenario.Grace is the human instructor who runs the platform. She does not have
time to read every learner’s feedback log, but she still needs to know when something in
the course itself is broken, not just when one learner is struggling.
Over the past week, the Operations & Quality Manager has been quietly comparing
error patterns across all active learners. It notices that fourteen different learners, at
different levels and with different coaches assigned, have all been marked down for the
same thing in their Writing feedback: misusing conditional sentences (“If I will go...”
instead of “If I go...”). That is unusual; normally only two or three learners share a weak
point at once, and the error is normally so basic that higher-band learners do not make
it. Here, even a band-7 learner made the mistake twice.
Thesystemdoesnotjustaddthistoalist; itcheckswhetherthepracticematerialitself
might be teaching the wrong thing. It looks at which prompts and example sentences the
Coach has been generating for conditional-sentence exercises, and finds that the LLM’s
own example sentences contain the same error in three of the last ten generated prompts.
That is the real problem: a flawed template is quietly misleading dozens of learners, not
a coincidence of individual mistakes.
Grace gets a single notification, not fourteen. It says what the pattern is, how many
learners are affected, what level they are at, and, because the system checked its own ma-
terial, that the root cause looks like a faulty example in the practice-generation prompt
rather than the learners. She reviews the flagged examples, confirms the system’s diag-
nosis, and fixes the template in one place. The next time a learner requests a conditional-
12

--- PAGE 14 ---
sentence exercise, the corrected version is used, and the Quality Manager keeps watching
to confirm the error rate actually drops before it closes the case.
Ad hoc requirements.
Watching for shared patterns
1. The Operations & Quality Manager continuously aggregates error tags (e.g., “con-
ditional sentence misuse”) across all active learners, not just within one learner’s
history.(what)
2. A pattern is only flagged as a possible curriculum gap once the number of learners
sharing it in a given week exceeds what is normal for that error type and learner
level, not on a fixed raw count.(how)
3. The system distinguishes an error that is expected at a learner’s level (e.g., article
misuse at band 4) from one that is unusual for it (the same error appearing at band
7), and weights the second kind as more suspicious.(how)
Telling material errors apart from learner errors
4. When a pattern is flagged, the system checks whether the error also appears in the
Coach’s own recently generated practice prompts and example sentences for that
topic.(what)
5. If the system’s own generated material contains the error, the notification names
the likely cause as the material rather than listing it as a learner weakness.(what)
6. The instructor is shown the specific flawed example sentences or prompts, not just
a description of the pattern, so she does not have to go hunting for them herself.
(how)
Keeping the instructor’s attention worth something
7. Grace receives one consolidated notification per distinct pattern, never one notifi-
cation per affected learner.(what)
8. The instructor should not be alerted more than roughly once a week for patterns
that turn out to be normal variation; most notifications she receives should reflect
a real, actionable issue.(how well)
9. Grace can approve the diagnosis, mark it as a false alarm, or request more learner
examples before deciding, and her response trains which future patterns get esca-
lated at the same sensitivity.(what)
Closing the loop

Can Trust(Suhani Saha)
Scenario.Suhani is a working professional who prepares for an IELTS or TOEFL exam.
Speaking is one of her weaker skills, and she wants to practise a realistic speaking task
without needing to wait for a private tutor. She asks the platform for a speaking task
based on her target score and recent weaknesses.
The system provides a suitable speech prompt and gives her preparation and speech
timers similar to the real examination. She records and submits her response. The system

then provides feedback on pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary, grammar, hesitations, and

Getting a speaking task
